# W2 — Identifying Premium and Trusted Services

## Objective

To identify the types of services that CityServe could offer and understand which services would provide real convenience and value to students and faculty at Thapar Institute.

## Work Done

During the second week, we focused more on **what kind of services CityServe should actually provide** instead of trying to include every possible local service.

One of the main points we discussed was that the platform should focus on services where **trust, quality and convenience** matter. For students and faculty, finding a reliable person for a small task can sometimes take a lot of time. We wanted CityServe to reduce that effort by bringing useful and verified services together in one place.

We discussed several examples of services that could be offered through the platform. Some of the ideas included:

* Fresh milk and other regular household requirements
* Premium laundry and dry-cleaning services
* Cloth alteration and repair services, including **rafoo/cloth fixing**
* Skilled electricians and plumbers
* Automobile repair and maintenance
* Mobile phone repair
* Laptop and computer repair
* Shoe repair and related services

The idea of **fresh milk delivery** was particularly useful during our discussion because it showed that CityServe does not necessarily have to be limited to repair or one-time services. Some services could be recurring and become part of a user's routine.

We also discussed having more specialised or **premium service options**. For example, instead of simply connecting someone with a laundry provider, users could potentially choose a more reliable service with better handling of clothes, pickup and delivery, and clearer pricing. Similar thinking could be applied to electronics, automobile repairs and other skilled services.

## Value for Students and Faculty

A major benefit we identified was **saving time**. Students living around the campus may not always have the time or knowledge to search for a good electrician, mechanic, tailor or repair technician themselves.

Having these services listed in one platform would make the process simpler. At the same time, verification and reviews could give users more confidence before placing a request.

For faculty members as well, having dependable local services available through a single platform could make everyday tasks more convenient.

## Key Learning

This week helped us understand that CityServe should not try to compete simply by having the largest number of services. Instead, the focus should be on providing **useful, reliable and convenient services** that people actually need.

We also realised that some services have more potential than others because they are either used frequently or involve a higher level of trust. Identifying these services early will help us decide what should be included in the initial version of CityServe and what can be added later.
