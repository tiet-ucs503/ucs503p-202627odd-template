# W1 — Understanding the Problem and Shaping CityServe

## Objective

To understand the actual problem we are trying to solve and narrow down our initial idea into something that can realistically be developed and tested.

## Work Done

This week, we mainly focused on discussing different possibilities for our project and figuring out what direction would make the most sense. After several discussions, we decided to work on **CityServe**, a platform intended to connect people with nearby service providers.

One thing we noticed early on was that finding a local service is usually not the difficult part. The bigger issue is knowing **which provider to trust, what the expected price should be, whether they are available, and what kind of experience other customers have had with them**.

This changed the way we looked at our project. Instead of making CityServe just another platform containing a list of shops and service providers, we wanted it to handle more of the actual interaction between the customer and the provider.

We therefore discussed features like searching for services, comparing different providers, checking their details and reviews, requesting a service, and eventually keeping track of the request.

## Choosing the Initial Target

We also discussed whether we should directly target an entire city or begin with a smaller community. We felt that trying to launch everywhere would make it difficult to properly understand the users and test our assumptions.

The Thapar campus and the surrounding area seemed like a better starting point. There is a relatively concentrated group of students, faculty and staff, and many of them depend on nearby services. This gives us an opportunity to understand their common problems without making the initial scope unnecessarily large.

## Reliability Considerations

While discussing the platform, I found that one of the biggest challenges would not necessarily be the technical implementation but maintaining the quality of the services listed on it.

For example, if someone books a service through CityServe and the provider does not show up, charges an unexpected amount, or provides poor service, the platform itself loses credibility. Because of this, we started considering things such as provider verification, customer ratings, reviews, clear pricing and feedback mechanisms.

These features could help users make better decisions instead of simply choosing the first provider they find.

## Key Learning

This week helped me understand that defining a project properly is just as important as implementing it. Our initial idea had many possible features, but discussing the actual user problem helped us decide what should matter most.

I also learned that **starting with a smaller and clearly defined user group can make it much easier to validate an idea**. Rather than trying to solve every local-service problem at once, we can first understand the needs around the Thapar ecosystem and then decide how the platform should grow from there.
