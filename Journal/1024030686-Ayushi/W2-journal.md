# W2 — Refining the Target Audience and Building the First Prototype

## Objective

The main objective for Week 2 was to turn our initial CityServe idea into a more focused project by defining the target audience, preparing the project proposal, and creating an early prototype of the platform.

## Work Done

During this week, we worked on formally presenting the CityServe concept through a project proposal. We prepared a PPT explaining the problem we were trying to address, our proposed solution, the users involved, and how the platform could work.

While preparing the proposal, we revisited our initial assumption about the target audience. Initially, we were thinking about building the platform for a much wider audience, where anyone could use CityServe to find local vendors and services.

However, we realised that trying to serve everyone from the beginning would make it difficult to understand user behaviour, validate the idea and build a reliable network of service providers. We therefore decided that it would be better to begin with a smaller and more clearly defined audience.

## Narrowing Down the Target Market

We started focusing on the **Thapar ecosystem and the areas surrounding it**, with students, faculty, staff and people living or working nearby forming our initial user base.

The idea was not simply to choose a smaller geographical area, but to start with a community whose needs we could actually observe and understand. Since we are familiar with the Thapar environment, it would also be easier for us to identify recurring problems and validate whether people would genuinely use the platform.

We also discussed the idea of targeting users who value **convenience, reliability and quality**, rather than trying to compete only by offering the lowest prices.

For example, someone may be willing to pay a little extra to get a daily essential such as fresh milk delivered reliably to their doorstep, if the source is trustworthy and the service saves them time. This helped us understand that the value of CityServe could come from the overall experience and reliability, not just from connecting users with the cheapest vendor.

## Expanding the Scope of Local Requirements

As we continued discussing the platform, we realised that local requirements are not limited to traditional service providers.

We considered a wider range of everyday needs that could potentially be supported by CityServe, including:

* Food and tiffin services
* Fresh milk and daily essentials
* Laundry and clothing care
* Tailoring and clothing alterations
* Rafu and repair services
* Electricians and plumbers
* Appliance repair
* Laptop and mobile repair
* Salons and grooming
* Automobile-related services
* Tutors
* Photography and event-related services

This helped us shape CityServe as a platform for **recurring local needs** rather than simply a website containing a list of vendors.

## Proposal and Website Prototype

Along with preparing the proposal PPT, we also started working on the first prototype of the CityServe website.

The purpose of the prototype was to visualise how a user would interact with the platform instead of keeping the idea limited to documentation. We started thinking about the main screens and the flow through which a user could discover a service, view available providers, compare options and proceed with a booking or order.

At this stage, the prototype was mainly used to communicate the concept and explore the possible user experience. It also helped us identify which features would be important for the eventual MVP.

We broadly separated the platform's transactions into two categories:

**Service-based transactions** — such as booking an electrician, plumber, salon appointment, repair service or tutor.

**Product/delivery-based transactions** — such as ordering milk, tiffin, groceries or other daily essentials.

This distinction helped us understand that different types of vendors may require different workflows even though they are part of the same ecosystem.

## Key Learning

Week 2 helped us understand the importance of **starting small and validating the idea before trying to scale it**.

Our thinking changed from trying to create a marketplace for a broad audience to first building a focused solution for the Thapar community and nearby areas. We also understood that users may choose a service not only because it is inexpensive, but because it is trustworthy, convenient and saves time.

Preparing the proposal PPT and developing the initial website prototype also helped us move from simply discussing the concept to actually visualising how CityServe could work as a product.

By the end of the week, CityServe had a clearer target audience, a more defined value proposition and an initial prototype that could be improved further in the coming weeks.
